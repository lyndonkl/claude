# Template: `acquisition` mode

The question is what we should pay for this target. The terminal artifact is a maximum
price with the synergy split shown.

Set the prior before the first number. Target shareholders capture nearly all the
announcement gain. Bidders capture roughly nothing and drift negative. A large share of
acquisition programs fail their own value tests, and many deals are divested within a
decade. The burden of proof belongs on the deal.

The report is built so a reader can see where the price came from and who captures it.

## Sections in order

### 1. Verdict

- The recommendation: proceed at or below a stated price, or walk away.
- The maximum justifiable price per share, and the price currently on the table.
- The four numbers, in a single small table: price, status quo value, restructured value,
  synergy value.
- Which of the three value reasons the deal rests on: undervaluation, control or synergy.
- The acid test result for that reason.
- The two or three assumptions the answer turns on.
- Whether the walk-away price was set before the auction.

### 2. Mandate, motive and chronology

The target, the acquirer, the currency, the valuation date. The stated motive.

Then the chronology, in dates: when the price was first discussed, when this valuation was
started, when it was finished. A valuation dated after the price is a rationalization, and
the reader can only see that if the dates are on the page.

### 3. The four numbers

Each number gets its own subsection, built in this order, because each is the baseline for
the next.

**Acquisition price.** For a public target, the pre-announcement market capitalization plus
the premium, in currency and percent. Decompose it: pre-deal book equity, plus
purchase-accounting intangibles, plus the market premium over book, plus the acquirer's
premium. Goodwill is the price less adjusted book equity, and it is a public promise to
create that much value.

**Status quo value.** A full discounted cash flow of the target as currently run. Use the
target's own cost of equity, the target's own business risk and the target's own debt
capacity. Show the bridge.

**Restructured value.** The same model with the investing, financing and payout changes in
place. Itemize every changed lever and the value each contributes. Control value is the
difference between this and the status quo — never a rule-of-thumb percentage. A perfectly
run target carries a control premium of zero, and the report should say so when that is
what the numbers show.

**Synergy value.** The combined firm with synergies, less the acquirer standalone plus the
**restructured** target. Using the status quo target here while also claiming control value
counts the control gain twice.

Verify and report the identity: the combined value with no synergy equals the sum of the
standalone values exactly. A mismatch means an assumption is inconsistent, and the report
should not proceed past it.

### 4. The synergy detail

Split cost synergies from revenue synergies and never blend them. Cost savings mostly
land. Revenue synergies mostly do not — around seven in ten mergers miss them.

For each claimed synergy, one row: the claim, the single valuation input it maps to, the
gross value, the haircut applied, the net value, and the year it starts. A claim that maps
to no input is a buzz word and belongs in a rejected list, not in the total. Diversification
is not a synergy for a public acquirer.

State the haircuts explicitly: customer attrition on revenue synergies, one-time costs to
achieve on the cost side, and the competitive haircut if you are one of several bidders.

### 5. The acid test

State the motive, then the test it must pass.

| Motive | The deal works only if |
|---|---|
| Undervaluation | price is below status quo value |
| Control | price is below restructured value |
| Synergy | price is below restructured plus synergy value |

If the price exceeds its benchmark, exactly two explanations survive. The synergy was
underestimated, or the acquirer is overpaying. Name which, and say what evidence decided it.

### 6. The seven-sin audit

A short table with a verdict per row and the rationalization offered where a row failed.

| Sin | What the report shows |
|---|---|
| Risk transference | the discount rate used, against the target's own cost of equity |
| Debt subsidy | the debt ratio and cost of debt used, against the target's own |
| Auto-pilot control premium | whether the premium came from a percentage or from the two runs |
| Elusive synergy | unquantified synergy currency against quantified |
| Relative pricing | whether any price or terminal value came from a multiple |
| Verdict first | the chronology from section 2 |
| No accountability | the named person whose pay depends on delivery |

### 7. Price, range and walk-away

The maximum justifiable price, with the range around it from the sensitivity on the two or
three assumptions that decide it. Then the walk-away price, and the statement that it was
set before the auction.

Show the split: at the offered price, how much of the control and synergy value goes to
the target's holders and how much remains with the acquirer. An acquirer that pays the full
value of control or the full synergy hands the entire gain to the seller, and the table
should make that visible.

State plainly that the split is negotiated. It is not a valuation output.

### 8. Deal design and the odds

The design factors that shift the base rate, each marked as favorable or not: sole bidder
against a bidding war, private or subsidiary target against a public one, cash against
stock, target size, cost synergies against growth synergies.

Note the interaction: large deals are dangerous for public targets, while private and
subsidiary deals have performed better as size rises.

### 9. Integration and accountability

The adoption lag assumed for each synergy. The named individual whose compensation depends
on the promised benefits arriving. Whether advisor pay is tied to deal performance or to
completion.

### 10. Unresolved findings, sources and vintages

See `disclosure-and-vintage.md`.

## What the reader will check

- The target is discounted at the target's own rate, not the acquirer's.
- The combined firm does not inherit a lower cost of capital purely from combining.
- No terminal value came from an exit multiple.
- Earnings accretion appears nowhere as a deal test. It follows mechanically whenever the
  acquirer trades at a higher earnings multiple than the target, and it carries no
  information about value.
- Control value, synergy value and any strategic premium are not stacked on one model.
