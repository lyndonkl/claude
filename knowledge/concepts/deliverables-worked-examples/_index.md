## How these concepts connect

Two deliverable templates anchor this area. [[corporate-finance-project-blueprint]] defines the ten-part corporate finance analysis; [[equity-valuation-project-blueprint]] defines the six-step equity valuation. Both start at [[project-company-selection]], and the two screens pull in opposite directions — the corporate finance project excludes money losers, while the valuation project requires one.

The corporate finance path runs strictly in order, because each part consumes the last. Governance and ownership come first: [[governance-analysis-deliverable]] establishes whether management will act on any recommendation, and [[stockholder-analysis-marginal-investor]] decides whether CAPM applies at all. Risk and return follow: [[regression-performance-diagnostics]] measures past performance while [[cost-of-capital-buildup-deliverable]] produces the hurdle rates. Those hurdle rates are then used twice — against accounting returns in [[return-spread-and-eva-analysis]], and as the starting point for financing. Financing runs qualitative first: [[qualitative-debt-tradeoff]] predicts where the optimum should sit, [[optimal-debt-ratio-wacc-schedule]] computes it, [[recapitalization-value-and-stress-test]] prices the move and tests whether it survives bad earnings, and [[debt-design-deliverable]] specifies how to get there and what the debt should look like. Cash return comes next in [[dividend-policy-deliverable]], which needs the return spreads to judge whether reinvestment beats payout. [[two-stage-fcff-company-valuation]] closes the loop, taking the optimal capital structure as its growth-phase discount rate and the fundamental growth rate from the reinvestment and ROC figures computed earlier. [[project-executive-summary-scorecard]] then compresses all ten parts onto one page.

The valuation path is a triangulation rather than a chain. [[dcf-model-selection]] fixes the model and assumptions, [[dcf-sensitivity-analysis]] bounds the answer, and three independent benchmarks follow — [[relative-valuation-comparables-regression]] against the sector, [[market-wide-multiple-regression]] against every listed firm, and [[equity-as-call-option-valuation]] only when the firm is both loss-making and levered above 50%. [[value-of-control-and-synergy]] adds the layers an acquirer would pay for. All of them land in [[valuation-triangulation-and-recommendation]], which reconciles the conflicting numbers into one call.

The two paths meet in three places. [[return-spread-and-eva-analysis]] is a required section of both. [[cost-of-capital-buildup-deliverable]] and [[optimal-debt-ratio-wacc-schedule]] feed the discount rates that every DCF in the valuation path depends on. And [[value-of-control-and-synergy]] is the valuation-side expression of exactly what the corporate finance project's capital-structure recommendations are worth in dollars.
