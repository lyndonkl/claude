## How these connect, and the order to use them

The area is one long argument with a single destination. It opens with the framework — [[first-principles-of-corporate-finance]] establishes that everything reduces to three decisions under one objective, and [[objective-function-and-stock-price-maximization]] explains why that objective defaults to the stock price. [[classical-objective-function-assumptions]] then states the four links that must hold for that default to be legitimate, and the rest of the area is a link-by-link audit. [[corporatism-archetypes]] runs alongside as the vocabulary for naming what a broken system looks like.

The audit takes the four links in order. Link I is the manager-stockholder conflict, worked through [[manager-stockholder-agency-conflict]], measured with [[board-independence-assessment]] and [[ownership-and-control-structure-analysis]], and evidenced by [[managerial-entrenchment-and-takeover-defenses]] and [[value-destroying-acquisitions]]. Link II is [[stockholder-bondholder-conflict]]. Link III is [[firms-and-financial-markets]]. Link IV is [[firms-and-society-social-costs]]. [[disney-governance-case-study]] is the running illustration that touches Links I, III and IV at once, and it is the best single template for how the audit reads on a real company.

Three candidate repairs follow. [[alternative-governance-systems]] is Solution I, [[alternative-objective-functions]] is Solution II, and [[self-correction-and-counter-forces]] is Solution III, which Damodaran endorses. [[governance-legislation-and-payoff]] supplies the evidence that governance pays and that legislating it does not work. Everything then resolves in [[modified-objective-function]], which converts the audit's findings into the one thing the rest of the course needs: whether to maximize stock price, stockholder wealth, or firm value.

**Practical order for an analyst assessing a real company:** (1) run the ownership and control analysis to find out who actually controls the firm; (2) grade the board; (3) inventory entrenchment provisions and the acquisition record; (4) read the covenants and check for expropriation; (5) test disclosure quality and market liquidity; (6) list material externalities; (7) check which counter-forces are live and unblocked; (8) name the archetype; (9) apply the modified objective function to fix the objective; (10) carry the resulting agency cost and constraint set into the valuation.
