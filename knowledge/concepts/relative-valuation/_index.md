## How these fit together

The area has a spine and two wings. The spine is Damodaran's four-step discipline. Start at [[pricing-vs-value]] to fix what a relative verdict can and cannot say, then run the multiple you intend to use through [[four-step-multiple-framework]]. Step one is [[multiple-definition-tests]]: settle the numerator, the denominator and the timing convention so every firm is measured the same way. Step two is [[multiple-distribution-statistics]]: find where the multiple actually sits across the market today, using medians rather than means, and note how many firms drop out. Step three is [[intrinsic-multiple-derivation]], which turns the multiple back into the DCF it came from and names its companion variable.

The first wing is that step-three machinery, one note per multiple family: [[intrinsic-pe-fundamentals]] for PE, [[peg-ratio]] for the modified version of it, [[book-value-multiples]] for PBV and EV/Invested Capital, [[ev-ebitda-multiple]] for cash-flow pricing, and [[ev-sales-and-brand-value]] for revenue pricing. Each tells you what to control for and what a "cheap" reading really requires.

The second wing is step four, application, ordered by how much control you need. [[comparable-selection-and-controls]] picks the peer set and the control technique. With one differing dimension you tell a story or use a modified multiple. With several you escalate to [[sector-regressions]] inside the peer group, or to [[market-wide-regressions]] against the whole market, or to [[cross-market-multiple-regressions]] when the company sits outside the US. [[industry-average-multiples]] supplies the sector anchors and companion-variable benchmarks these steps need. Two special cases branch off: [[country-pe-regression]] and [[market-pe-vs-bond-alternative]] apply the same logic to entire markets rather than firms, and [[pricing-young-companies]] handles the case where the conventional fundamentals are negative or meaningless.

A typical firm-level pass runs in seven moves. Define the multiple. Locate it in the distribution. Derive its companion variable. Choose comparables and a control technique. Compute the predicted multiple. Compare it with the traded multiple. Then state the verdict as relative, not absolute.
