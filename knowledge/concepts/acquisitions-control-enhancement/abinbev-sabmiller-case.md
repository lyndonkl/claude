# AB InBev / SABMiller: an end-to-end deal valuation

**Core idea:** This is the integrating case for the whole acquisition framework. A real mega-deal goes through the full sequence. Profile both firms. Value the target as currently run. Benchmark it to find control potential. Revalue it restructured. Build the combined firm with and without synergy. Then apply the acid test to the price. The answer comes out negative by roughly $33 billion, which forces the analyst into an honest choice — either the synergy estimate is far too conservative, or a respected acquirer overpaid. The case teaches the discipline of finishing the arithmetic before deciding what you believe.

**Formulas:** All standard, applied in sequence.
- `Expected growth = Reinvestment rate × After-tax return on capital`.
- `Value of equity = Value of operating assets + Cash + Minority holdings − Debt − Minority interests`.
- `Value of control = Optimal equity value − Status quo equity value`.
- `Value of synergy = Combined firm with synergy − Combined firm status quo`.
- Acid test: `Price < Restructured value + Synergy value` for a control-plus-synergy deal.
- `Premium to justify = Price − Pre-announcement market capitalization`.

**Procedure (the sequence the case follows):**
1. Profile the acquirer. Capital mix, operating metrics, and geographic revenue split. Look for the strategic gap the deal is meant to fill.
2. Profile the target the same way.
3. State the merger motives and classify them: undervaluation, control, or synergy.
4. Value the target status quo, in parts if it holds JVs and associates. Compare to market cap to test the undervaluation motive.
5. Benchmark the target against the acquirer and the sector on margin, tax rate, ROIC, reinvestment rate and debt ratio.
6. Build the restructured (optimal) case from those gaps and compute the value of control.
7. Build the combined firm as a weighted composite, using the target's *restructured* values, and verify the no-synergy combined value equals the sum of the parts.
8. Overlay the synergy assumptions and revalue. Subtract to get synergy value.
9. Apply the acid test against the actual price. Report the gap and name the two possible explanations.

**Worked example — the full chain:**

*Step 1, the strategic gap.* AB InBev earns 0% of revenue in Africa. SABMiller earns 31% there. That single pair of numbers explains the complementarity motive.

*Step 2, status quo.* SABMiller is valued in three pieces — the parent, the Coors JV stake, and the share of associates — each with its own cost of capital, ROC and reinvestment rate. Consolidated operating assets are $64,566.26M. Add cash of $1,027M, subtract debt of $12,918M and minority interests of $1,183M, giving equity of **$51,492.26M**. The parent's own growth is `16.02% × 10.33% = 1.65%`, very low because it reinvests very little. Against a $75B market price, SABMiller was **not undervalued**. Motive 1 fails.

*Step 3, control potential.* Benchmarking finds SABMiller behind on almost every metric.

| Metric | SABMiller | AB InBev | Sector |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-tax operating margin | 19.97% | 32.28% | 19.23% |
| Effective tax rate | 26.36% | 18.00% | 22.00% |
| Pre-tax ROIC | 14.02% | 14.76% | 17.16% |
| ROIC | 10.33% | 12.10% | 13.38% |
| Reinvestment rate | 16.02% | 50.99% | 33.29% |
| Debt to capital | 14.67% | 23.38% | 18.82% |

Every gap is a lever.

*Step 4, restructured value.* Raise ROC to 12.64% and the reinvestment rate to the sector's 33.29%. Growth rises from 1.65% to `0.3329 × 0.1264 = 4.21%`. More debt raises the cost of equity to 9.37% but trims WACC to 8.03%. Equity value rises to **$56,194.44M**. Value of control = `56,194.44 − 51,492.26 = $4,702.17M`. Motive 2 also fails: `$51.5B + $4.7B = $56.2B`, still far below $75B.

*Step 5, the combined firm.* Combine AB InBev with the *restructured* SABMiller. Revenues add to $67,892M. The blended operating margin is 28.27%, ROC 11.68%, reinvestment rate 43.58%, growth 5.09%, WACC 7.51%. Combined operating assets: **$262,018M**. Check the arithmetic: `211,953 + 50,065 = 262,018`. Adding two firms creates nothing, exactly as required.

*Step 6, synergy.* Overlay the synergy assumptions — operating margin to 30.00% (cost synergies from Latin American consolidation), ROC to 12.00%, reinvestment rate to 50.00%, growth to 6.00%. EBIT rises from $19,191.33M to $20,368M. Combined operating assets become **$276,610M**. Value of synergy = `276,610 − 262,018 = $14,592M`, about $14.6B. Note the cost of capital is 7.51% in both columns — the synergy runs entirely through operations, not financing.

*Step 7, the acid test.* `$56.2B + $14.6B = $70.8B` against a $104B price. The deal fails by about $33B.

*Step 8, the honest conclusion.* Exactly two explanations survive. Either the synergy potential was massively underestimated — higher margins or higher growth than assumed — or AB InBev significantly overpaid. Overpayment would run against its own history as a disciplined acquirer and against 3G Capital's reputation as a steward of capital. The case does not resolve the tension; it insists you state it.

**Determinism:**
- DETERMINISTIC: every ratio in the profiles. Operating margin comes from revenues and EBIT; debt ratios from debt and market cap. Also deterministic: the whole status-quo DCF given its inputs, the growth identity `RR × ROC`, the equity bridge, the value-of-control subtraction, the weighted-average combined inputs, the synergy subtraction, the acid-test comparisons, and the premium arithmetic.
- JUDGMENT: the operating and risk assumptions in each DCF (betas, ERPs by geography, growth-period length, stable growth). Which benchmark gaps are closeable and by how much. Every synergy assumption — the 30% margin, the 50% reinvestment rate, the 6% growth. Whether the residual $33B gap means conservative synergy estimates or overpayment.

**Pitfalls:**
- Combining the acquirer with the target's *status quo* value while separately claiming control value. Use the restructured target, as the case does, or control gains get counted twice.
- Skipping the sum-of-parts check on the no-synergy combined value.
- Letting the combined firm inherit a lower cost of capital simply from combining.
- Treating the market's $33B addition to combined market value as confirmation. That is a price reaction, not a value estimate, and the deal analysis exists precisely to test it.
- Reverse-engineering the synergy assumptions until the value reaches $104B. That is the verdict-first sin, and the case is designed to tempt you into it.
- Forgetting the deal consequences — selling MillerCoors and the Chinese segment — which change the asset base being valued.
