## How these connect, and the order to use them

The area splits into three layers that stack.

**Layer 1 — priors and process discipline.** Start with `acquisition-empirical-record` to set the base rate: acquirers usually destroy value, and the failure is structural. `seven-sins-of-acquisitions` names the seven structural defects and gives the audit scorecard. Five of the sins have their own concept note explaining the mechanics and the fix: `target-discount-rate-discipline` (sins 1 and 2), `control-premium-rules-of-thumb` (sin 3), `synergy-taxonomy` plus `valuing-synergy` (sin 4), `transaction-and-exit-multiples` (sin 5), and `deal-bias-and-ego` (sins 6 and 7). `acquisition-price-buildup-and-goodwill` shows what the sins cost when they compound. `acquisition-strategy-design` and `synergy-delivery-odds` turn the evidence into forward-looking deal design.

**Layer 2 — the valuation machinery.** `three-reasons-and-acid-test` is the spine. It says there are only three value reasons for a deal, and it requires four numbers. Build those numbers in order: `status-quo-valuation` first (which also settles the undervaluation question), then `restructured-value-and-value-of-control`, then `valuing-synergy` on top of the restructured target. Apply the acid test. `abinbev-sabmiller-case` runs the entire sequence end to end on a real deal and is the best single worked reference for the layer.

**Layer 3 — control outside acquisitions, and value enhancement.** The status-quo/optimal pair from layer 2 is reusable well beyond M&A. `expected-value-of-control` multiplies the value gap by the probability of change. From there, `implied-probability-of-management-change` reads that probability out of a market price, and `voting-premium-and-minority-discount` allocates control value across share classes and private stakes. Separately, `paths-to-value-creation` supplies the only four levers a restructuring plan can pull, and `growth-quality-and-excess-returns` decides whether growth is even worth pursuing (for the majority of firms, ROIC is below WACC and it is not). `eva-and-dcf-equivalence` proves that the popular value-enhancement metric is the same model in different clothing, and `gaming-eva` shows how managing to it year-to-year goes wrong.

**Typical analyst path for a live deal:** empirical record → seven sins audit → status quo → benchmark and restructure → value of control → synergy → acid test → deal-design and delivery review. **For a governance or activist question:** status quo → restructure → expected value of control → implied probability → share-class or stake pricing. **For an internal value-enhancement program:** paths to value creation → growth quality → EVA equivalence → EVA gaming checks.
