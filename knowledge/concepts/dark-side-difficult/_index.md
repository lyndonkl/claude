# Dark side of valuation: difficult companies — concept index

One line per concept: slug — what it covers — determinism flag.

- `difficult-company-taxonomy` — the four fundamental questions of any valuation, and the routing table from a company's difficulty (young / mature in transition / declining / emerging-market / financial / cyclical / intangible-heavy) to the repair it needs — JUDGMENT
- `young-company-valuation` — full procedure for a negative-earnings firm: work backwards from a mature end-state, margin convergence, the NOL tax engine, fading discount rates, bottom-up betas; Amazon 2000/2019/2020 and the fcffneg.xls model — DETERMINISTIC given assumptions, JUDGMENT on the end-state
- `sales-to-capital-reinvestment` — reinvestment from the revenue change, invested-capital rollforward, imputed ROC as a consistency check, terminal RIR = g/ROC — DETERMINISTIC
- `dilution-and-employee-options` — why a DCF already contains dilution, and the dilution-adjusted Black-Scholes for existing options — DETERMINISTIC (with judgment on volatility and grant terms)
- `value-of-control-and-restructuring` — status quo versus optimally run, the operating and financial restructuring levers, the optimal-debt-ratio table, ROIC > WACC as the value test, P(management change) — DETERMINISTIC given inputs, JUDGMENT on the optimal policy and the probability
- `return-improvement-and-governance-drag` — quantifying weak governance as low returns; the marginal-return lever versus the existing-asset efficiency-growth lever; Tube Investments' three scenarios — DETERMINISTIC given inputs, JUDGMENT on the target ROC
- `declining-firm-valuation` — negative revenue growth, margin recovery to the sector median, negative reinvestment as capital is released; JC Penney and Heineken — DETERMINISTIC given assumptions
- `distress-and-failure-adjusted-value` — the probability-weighted blend of going-concern and distress values, distress proceeds as a percent of book or fair value, sector survival tables — DETERMINISTIC blend, JUDGMENT on probability and recovery
- `bond-implied-distress-probability` — inverting a traded bond price for the annual and cumulative probability of distress; distress.xls and Las Vegas Sands — DETERMINISTIC
- `country-risk-exposure` — country risk by exposure not by passport: revenue-weighted equity risk premiums and lambdas; the Tata companies, Embraer, Heineken, and the Jan-2021 country ERP table — DETERMINISTIC given lambda/weights, JUDGMENT on lambda
- `currency-consistency-and-invariance` — value is the same in any currency; building a riskfree rate from an inflation differential; PPP exchange rates; negative riskfree rates; Infosys in rupees and dollars — DETERMINISTIC given consistent inputs
- `cross-holdings` — why half the value of a group company can sit outside the DCF, and how to add stakes and subtract minority interests; the Tata value breakdown — DETERMINISTIC bridge, JUDGMENT on each holding's value
- `truncation-and-political-risk` — nationalization, regime change and catastrophe as probability-weighted scenarios; Aramco valued on promised dividends, on FCFE, and under regime change — DETERMINISTIC blend, JUDGMENT on the probability
- `financial-service-firm-valuation` — why FCFF fails for banks, the dividend discount model as the default, sustainable ROE after a regulatory capital increase; Wells Fargo and CIB Egypt — DETERMINISTIC given inputs, JUDGMENT on sustainable ROE
- `bank-fcfe-and-excess-return-models` — book value and regulatory capital as a bank's reinvestment; FCFE = net income − investment in Tier 1 capital; the equity excess-return model; Deutsche Bank and eqexret.xls — DETERMINISTIC given the capital and ROE paths
- `capitalizing-rd` — converting R&D (and recruiting, and brand advertising) into an asset; the amortizable-life table; restating income, capital and returns; Amgen and Boeing — DETERMINISTIC
- `normalized-earnings` — the three normalization approaches for a cyclical or temporarily troubled firm, plus the synthetic-rating chain that follows; normearn.xls — DETERMINISTIC given the approach, JUDGMENT on whether normalization is legitimate
- `commodity-and-cyclical-valuation` — separating macro from micro: value at today's commodity price, regress revenues on the price, normalize margins and ROC; Shell at $40 oil — DETERMINISTIC given the price and anchors
- `scenario-analysis-and-simulation` — two-way sensitivity tables and Monte Carlo; input distributions, correlations, percentile output; Amazon, Shell and the S&P 500 — DETERMINISTIC mechanics, JUDGMENT on distributions
- `value-versus-price` — pricing versus valuing, the determinants of price, the three camps, margin of safety and catalysts, converting a gap into an expected return; Apple, Amazon and the forecast retrospective — JUDGMENT, with a deterministic gap-to-return calculation
- `market-and-macro-crisis-valuation` — valuing the whole index with augmented dividends, and revaluing a company when macro inputs move; the S&P 500 in Jan 2020 and Nov 2020, 3M before and after Lehman, and the 2017 tax reform — DETERMINISTIC given assumptions

## How these fit together

Start at `difficult-company-taxonomy`. It asks the four questions every valuation answers and tells you which of them the company in front of you breaks, which in turn names the concept you need next. From there the area splits along the life cycle and then by market and sector.

**Life cycle.** For a young, money-losing firm, `young-company-valuation` is the spine — work backwards from a mature end-state — with `sales-to-capital-reinvestment` charging for the growth and `dilution-and-employee-options` closing the equity bridge. For a mature firm where change is expected or forced, `value-of-control-and-restructuring` values it twice and weights the two by the probability of change; `return-improvement-and-governance-drag` is the same machinery aimed at a firm whose returns sit below its cost of capital. For a firm in decline, `declining-firm-valuation` handles negative growth and negative reinvestment, and hands off to `distress-and-failure-adjusted-value` whenever survival is genuinely in doubt. `bond-implied-distress-probability` supplies the sharpest input that concept needs.

**Market.** Emerging-market work is a stack of four adjustments applied on top of an ordinary DCF: `country-risk-exposure` for the discount rate, `currency-consistency-and-invariance` for the unit of account, `cross-holdings` for everything the DCF does not cover, and `truncation-and-political-risk` for the catastrophic tail. `return-improvement-and-governance-drag` usually belongs in that stack too.

**Sector.** Banks and insurers get their own models — `financial-service-firm-valuation` for the dividend route and `bank-fcfe-and-excess-return-models` when payout no longer reflects capacity. Intangible-heavy firms need `capitalizing-rd` **before** any valuation, since it changes earnings, capital and returns. Cyclical and commodity firms need `normalized-earnings` or `commodity-and-cyclical-valuation`, depending on whether a usable price driver exists.

**Closing.** Every one of these produces a point estimate that should not be trusted as one. `scenario-analysis-and-simulation` converts it into a distribution and tests whether the market's price is merely possible or actually probable. `market-and-macro-crisis-valuation` supplies the macro inputs and shows that your own intrinsic value moves when they do. `value-versus-price` is where the work ends: it separates valuing from pricing, turns the gap into an expected return, and asks the question that decides whether the analysis is worth acting on — will the gap close, and what makes it close?

Typical order for a single hard company: classify (taxonomy) → clean the accounts (capitalize R&D, capitalize leases, normalize earnings if the trouble is temporary) → build the base DCF with the life-cycle-appropriate concept → layer market and sector adjustments (country risk, currency, cross holdings, regulatory capital) → apply truncation or distress weighting → simulate → compare with price and name the catalyst.
