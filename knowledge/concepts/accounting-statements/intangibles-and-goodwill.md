# Intangible assets and goodwill

**Core idea:** Accountants talk a big game on intangibles. From the talk you would think they know how to value brand name and management quality. In reality they are good only at small-bore intangibles such as licenses and customer lists, where the earnings and cash flows are easy to see and forecast. They remain poor at the big ones. Most intangible value on balance sheets worldwide is a single item: goodwill. Goodwill is a plug variable that signifies little. It appears only when a company makes an acquisition. It equals the price paid minus the target's dressed-up book value, and it exists as an asset only because without it the balance sheet would not balance.

**Formulas:**
- `Goodwill = Price paid for the target − Target's restated ("dressed up") book value of assets`. Price paid = what the buyer paid in the deal. Restated book value = the target's asset book value, restated at the time of the deal.
- `Net carrying value of a definite-lived intangible = Gross carrying value − Accumulated amortization`.
- Indefinite-lived intangibles are not amortized: `Carrying value = Original recorded value − Cumulative impairments`.
- Impairment test outcome: if the re-estimated value of the acquired target has risen, goodwill is unchanged; if it has fallen, goodwill is written down by the shortfall.
- Accounting-intangible coverage check: `Accounting intangibles / Market value of equity`. A tiny ratio at a brand- or R&D-driven firm shows how little of the real intangible value accounting captures.

**Reference data:** The two classes of accounting intangible.

| Class | Examples | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Indefinite-lived | Trademarks, bottlers' franchise rights, goodwill | Not amortized; tested for impairment annually |
| Definite-lived | Customer relationships, some franchise rights and trademarks, licenses | Amortized over the assumed life; carried at gross less accumulated amortization |

Goodwill rule history:

| Era | Rule |
|---|---|
| Most of the last century | Goodwill written off on autopilot, often amortized over long periods in equal installments |
| Late 1990s onward (GAAP and IFRS) | Annual impairment test replaces automatic amortization; accountants revisit the estimate and judge whether value fell |

**Procedure:**
1. Split the intangible section into indefinite-lived and definite-lived, using the intangibles footnote.
2. Isolate goodwill. Trace it to the acquisitions that created it. Goodwill can exist only where there was a deal.
3. For definite-lived intangibles, record gross carrying value, accumulated amortization and net carrying value. The net figure is what appears on the face of the balance sheet.
4. Test the coverage. Compare total accounting intangibles with your independent view of the firm's intangible value. At a brand company or a pharma firm, the accounting figure will be a sliver.
5. Read the impairment history. Count how many years the firm has impaired goodwill and by how much. Repeated impairment is a record of serial overpayment, not a one-time event.
6. For valuation, strip goodwill out of invested capital if you want a return on capital that reflects the operating business. Leave it in if you want a return that holds management accountable for acquisition prices. Say which you did.
7. Do not treat an impairment as new information about value. Impairments track sector pricing moves and lag them by months or years.

**Worked example:** Coca-Cola, 2019 ($ millions). Definite-lived intangibles net to just 517 — gross 917 less accumulated amortization 400. Coca-Cola's worth rests almost entirely on its brand. The accounting system has captured 517 million of it in the amortizable column. A further 9,266 sits in indefinite-lived trademarks, and those came from acquisitions rather than from a century of brand building. Goodwill of 16,764 dwarfs both, and it is a residue of deal prices, not a measure of the Coca-Cola brand. Dr. Reddy's shows the same gap in pharma: goodwill 323 million rupees, other intangibles 6,318, intangibles under development 277, on total assets of 194,758. The firm's real value is a drug pipeline built by R&D that accounting expenses rather than capitalizes.

**Determinism:**
- DETERMINISTIC: goodwill as price paid minus restated book value; net carrying value from gross less accumulated amortization; all the subtotals; the coverage ratio.
- JUDGMENT: the "dressing up" restatement of the target's assets at deal time; the annual re-valuation that drives the impairment test; the estimate of what the firm's intangibles are truly worth; the choice of whether to include goodwill in invested capital. These need the acquisition footnote, the impairment disclosure, and an independent valuation of the acquired business.

**Pitfalls:**
- Reading goodwill as a measure of brand value or franchise strength. It is a plug that makes the balance sheet balance.
- Reading a goodwill impairment as market-moving news. Its effect on stock prices has been negligible, because it lags the market's own repricing.
- Assuming that indefinite-lived means permanent. Those assets are impairment-tested and can be written down.
- Assuming that a firm with small accounting intangibles has few intangible assets. The reverse is common: R&D and brand spending are expensed, so the most intangible-heavy firms often show the least.
- Comparing goodwill across an acquisitive firm and an organic grower as though it were the same kind of asset.
