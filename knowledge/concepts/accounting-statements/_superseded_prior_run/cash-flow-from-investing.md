# Cash flow from investing: operating vs non-operating assets

**Core idea:** The investing section nets the cash a firm puts into, and pulls out of, long-lived assets. It mixes two groups. Investment in operating assets is cap ex net of asset sales, plus cash spent buying other firms. It is the engine that drives growth in revenues and operating income. Investment in non-operating assets covers financial assets, stakes, and other side holdings. These create a separate source of value. Their payoff shows up below the operating income line, as income from cross holdings or securities. One carve-out: liquid, close-to-riskless securities count as cash and marketable securities, not investing. And one blind spot: deals paid for with stock never appear here, since no cash changes hands.

**Formulas:** Cash Flow from Investing = − Capital Expenditures + Divestitures of assets − Cash Acquisitions − Investments in financial assets − Investments in non-operating assets + Divestitures of securities and non-operating assets. (Outflows are minuses; inflows from selling are pluses.)

**Procedure:**
1. Sum the section with signs: cap ex and cash purchases negative, sale proceeds positive.
2. Split the lines into operating investment (cap ex, asset sales, cash purchases of firms) and non-operating investment (financial assets and their sales).
3. Apply the carve-out: buying liquid, near-riskless securities is cash management, not real investing. Strip those lines when measuring reinvestment.
4. Check for reinvestment hiding elsewhere. Some firms' true capital spending runs through operations (Netflix's content). Deals paid in stock appear nowhere.
5. Read growth sources: internal (cap ex) vs external (buying firms). Only operating investment feeds the operating line items.

**Reference data:** Section build-up:

| Sign | Item | Group |
|---|---|---|
| − | Capital Expenditures | Operating assets |
| + | Divestitures of assets | Operating assets |
| − | Cash Acquisitions | Operating assets |
| − | Investments in financial assets | Non-operating assets |
| − | Investments in non-operating assets | Non-operating assets |
| + | Divestitures of securities and non-operating assets | Non-operating assets |
| = | Cash Flow from Investing | |

**Worked example:** Coca-Cola 2019 ($ millions). Investing used (3,976), but the mix is telling. Purchases of businesses, equity-method stakes and nonmarketable securities took (5,542). PP&E purchases took only (2,054). Coke grows by buying, not building. Dr. Reddy's FY2020 (₹ millions) shows the carve-out problem. Its "investing" holds purchases of investments of (122,726), mostly offset by sale proceeds of 109,186. That is cash parked in financial assets, not real reinvestment. True physical spending was only (4,262) of PP&E plus (476) of intangibles. Total S.A. 2019 shows routine asset sales: $2,060M of disposals against $11,810M of PP&E and intangible additions.

**Determinism:** DETERMINISTIC: the signed sum, once lines are labeled. JUDGMENT: sorting operating from non-operating investment, deciding what counts as cash-equivalent parking rather than investing, and finding reinvestment that sits outside the section (content in operations, stock-funded deals off the statement).

**Pitfalls:** Reading the section total as "reinvestment" when much of it is securities churn. Missing that stock-funded acquisitions never hit the statement. Missing capital spending booked in operations (content, capitalized development). Expecting non-operating investments to boost operating income — their payoff lands below that line.
