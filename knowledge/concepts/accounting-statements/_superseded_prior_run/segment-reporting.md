# Segment and geographic revenue breakdowns

**Core idea:** A firm that spans many businesses and regions reports one group income statement. That total hides very different economics underneath. So firms break revenues down two ways: by region and by business segment. The breakdown sometimes sits in the income statement, but more often in the footnotes. Segment tables also give operating income, assets, and capital spending per segment. Reading them shows where the money is truly made. That can be far from where the revenue is booked. Segment labels involve judgment by the firm, so the labels differ across firms and over time.

**Formulas:** Consolidated total = Sum of segment values + Corporate items − Intersegment eliminations. (Eliminations remove sales between segments so nothing is double counted.)

**Procedure:**
1. Find the segment note in the footnotes (or, rarely, in the income statement itself).
2. Check what the segments are: geographic, business-line, or a mix. Note the firm chose these labels.
3. Check the tie-out: segment revenues plus corporate, minus eliminations, must equal the group total.
4. Compare segment operating margins (segment operating income / segment revenues). Flag segments with fat revenues but thin profits, and the reverse.
5. Compare asset weight: segment assets and capital spending against segment operating income.
6. Use the mix to judge risk: how much rides on one region, one commodity, or one business line.

**Worked example:** Total S.A. 2019. Refining & Chemicals has the biggest revenues ($116,973M) but thin operating income ($3,342M — a 2.9% margin). Exploration & Production has modest revenues ($38,590M) but the biggest operating income ($10,542M — a 27% margin). The group statement alone would hide this. The upstream business earns the money; the downstream business moves the volume. Tie-out: net operating income 13,436 − net cost of net debt 1,998 − non-controlling interests 171 = net income group share 11,267. For Coca-Cola, note Bottling Investments carries $11,170M of assets for only $358M of operating income, while Europe/ME/Africa earns $3,551M on $8,143M of assets.

**Determinism:** DETERMINISTIC: the tie-out of segment values (with eliminations) to consolidated totals, and per-segment margin and capital-intensity ratios. JUDGMENT: how the firm drew its segment lines, and what the mix says about regional economics and risk.

**Pitfalls:** Assuming segment labels are standard — they are the firm's choice. Ignoring intersegment sales and eliminations, which double count if missed. Judging a segment by revenues alone when its margin or asset load tells the opposite story. Missing that "Corporate" often holds interest income and expense plus unallocated costs.
