# Debt and current liabilities on the balance sheet

**Core idea:** Current liabilities break into three groups, and the split matters for analysis. Group 1: non-interest-bearing items such as accounts payable and supplier credit — part of normal operations. Group 2: interest-bearing short-term borrowings — commercial paper, short-term debt, and the part of long-term debt due within a year. Group 3: deferred salaries, taxes, and other short-term dues. Rule: when computing non-cash working capital, leave interest-bearing short-term debt out of current liabilities and move it into debt. Debt itself takes three forms: corporate bonds (public markets), bank loans, and lease debt. Since 2019, operating lease commitments count as debt too; before that, only capital leases did. Unlike the asset side, the liability side has resisted mark-to-market: bank debt sits at the amount first borrowed, and bonds due are mostly not marked to market. The balance sheet gives only totals; the footnotes give each issue's rate, maturity and features, plus a year-by-year table of when payments come due.

**Formulas:**
- Total debt = Short-term interest-bearing debt + Current maturities of long-term debt + Long-term debt (+ lease debt since 2019).
- Non-cash working capital = Non-cash current assets − Non-debt current liabilities (see [[cash-flow-from-operations]]).

**Procedure:**
1. Sort each current liability into the three groups: operating (payables, supplier credit), interest-bearing borrowing, and deferred items.
2. Re-sort: pull interest-bearing short-term debt out of current liabilities and add it to debt. Never leave it in working capital.
3. Sum total debt: short-term borrowings + current portion of long-term debt + long-term debt + lease debt.
4. Go to the footnotes for detail. Read each issue's stated rate, due date, fixed vs floating, straight vs convertible.
5. Pull the due-date table (payments owed year by year). Use it to judge rollover risk and to compute debt duration.
6. Remember book debt is close to what is owed (it is one of the two trustworthy balance-sheet blocks), but it is not marked to market.

**Worked example:** Coca-Cola 2019. Balance-sheet current liabilities of $26,973M include loans and notes payable of $10,994M and current maturities of long-term debt of $4,253M — both interest-bearing, so both move to debt for analysis. Total interest-bearing debt: 10,994 + 4,253 + 27,516 = $42,763M. Accounts payable and accrued expenses of $11,312M stay in working capital. The footnote table shows borrowing across four currencies at a blended 1.9%, with maturities out to 2098 — the large, complex debt of a mature firm.

**Determinism:** DETERMINISTIC: the group sorting once line items are labeled, the total-debt sum, and reading rates and maturities from footnotes. JUDGMENT: spotting debt-like items beyond the labeled lines (lease commitments pre-2019, other contractual dues), and judging refinancing risk from the maturity table.

**Pitfalls:** Leaving interest-bearing short-term debt inside working capital — it skews both working capital and debt ratios. Missing lease debt, above all in statements from before 2019. Assuming book debt equals the market value of debt; the liability side is not marked to market. Stopping at the balance-sheet total when the footnotes hold the rate and due-date detail.
