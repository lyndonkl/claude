# Income statement structure

**Core idea:** The income statement is a ladder. It starts at revenues and steps down to net income, netting out one class of expense at each rung. Each rung is a profit measure with its own meaning. Gross profit measures production profitability. Operating profit measures the profitability of the business itself. Taxable income is what equity investors earn before tax. Net income is what they earn after tax. Knowing which rung you are on tells you whose claim has already been paid and whose has not.

**Formulas:**
- `Gross Profit = Revenues − Cost of Goods Sold`
- `Operating Profit (EBIT) = Gross Profit − Other Operating Expenses`
- `Taxable Income (Pre-tax Income) = Operating Profit − Financial Expenses`
- `Net Income = Taxable Income − Taxes`
- Collapsed form: `Operating Profit = Revenues − Operating Expenses`, where Operating Expenses = COGS + Other Operating Expenses.
- `Basic EPS = Net Income to common / Weighted-average basic shares`; `Diluted EPS = Net Income to common / Weighted-average diluted shares`.
- `Comprehensive Income = Net Income + Other Comprehensive Income`.
- `Net Income to parent = Consolidated Net Income − Income to noncontrolling interests`.

Symbols: Revenues = the accountant's count of sales generated by transactions during the period. COGS = expenses of producing the products or services behind top-line sales. Other Operating Expenses = SG&A and other costs of running the firm, not tied directly to producing the goods. Financial Expenses = expenses of using non-equity capital, mainly interest on debt. Taxes = taxes due on taxable income, which need not equal cash taxes paid. Noncontrolling (minority) interests = the share of a consolidated subsidiary's income owned by outsiders.

**Reference data:** The canonical ladder.

| Step | Item | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| Start with | Revenues | Accountant's estimate of sales generated by transactions during the period |
| Net out | Cost of Goods Sold | Expenses of producing the products/services that represent top-line sales |
| To get | **Gross Profit** | Production profitability |
| Net out | Other Operating Expenses | SG&A and other operating costs not directly tied to producing goods and services |
| To get | **Operating Profit** | Profitability of the business |
| Net out | Financial Expenses | Expenses associated with use of non-equity capital, especially debt |
| To get | **Taxable Income** | Income for equity investors, prior to taxes |
| Net out | Taxes | Taxes due on taxable income |
| To get | **Net Income** | Income for equity investors, after taxes |

**Procedure:**
1. Copy the filing's income statement lines verbatim, with the reporting unit and currency noted.
2. Sort every expense line into COGS, other operating, or financial. Use [[expense-classification-and-depreciation]] for the rules.
3. Compute gross profit. If the filer gives no COGS line, build a proxy and say so.
4. Compute operating profit. Push anything that is not an operating expense of this period below the line: interest, equity income, FX gains, one-time items.
5. Compute taxable income, then net income. Reconcile net income to the filer's reported figure. Any gap means an item was mis-sorted.
6. If the filing reports consolidated net income and a noncontrolling-interest line, subtract the NCI to get income to the parent's shareholders. Use that figure for EPS and for equity valuation.
7. Compute basic and diluted EPS from the weighted-average share counts in the filing. The dilutive gap tells you how much option and convertible overhang exists.
8. If other comprehensive income is material, note it. Translation adjustments and unrealized securities gains live there, not in net income.

**Worked example:** Coca-Cola, 2019 ($ millions). Net operating revenues 37,266 − COGS 14,619 = gross profit 22,647. Subtract SG&A 12,103 and other operating charges 458 → operating income 10,086. Add interest income 563, subtract interest expense 946, add equity income 1,049 and other income 34 → income before income taxes 10,786. Subtract income taxes 1,801 → consolidated net income 8,985. Subtract noncontrolling interests 65 → net income to Coca-Cola shareowners 8,920. Basic EPS = 8,920 / 4,276M shares = $2.09; diluted EPS = 8,920 / 4,314M = $2.07. The 38M share gap is the effect of dilutive securities.

**Determinism:**
- DETERMINISTIC: every subtraction in the ladder. Inputs = revenues, COGS, other operating expenses, financial expenses, taxes, NCI, share counts. Outputs = gross profit, operating profit, taxable income, net income, income to parent, basic and diluted EPS, comprehensive income. A script computes all of these.
- JUDGMENT: sorting each reported line into the right rung. This needs the accounting-policy footnote, the segment note, and knowledge of the business. Judgment also decides whether an item that the filer put above the operating line truly belongs there.

**Pitfalls:**
- Treating the filer's "operating income" label as authoritative. Firms differ on what sits above the line.
- Using consolidated net income where income to parent shareholders is required. The NCI slice does not belong to your shareholders.
- Forgetting that the tax line is book tax, not cash tax. Deferred taxes make the two diverge.
- Comparing gross margins across firms whose COGS definitions differ. SG&A in particular is a vague catch-all that can hold almost anything.
- Ignoring the basic-versus-diluted share gap when it is large, as it often is at young firms.
