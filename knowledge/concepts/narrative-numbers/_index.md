## How these connect, and the order an analyst uses them

The area has a spine and two rings. The spine is the story process. Start with the mindset concepts — `valuation-misconceptions` and `bermuda-triangle-of-valuation` — because they set what a valuation can and cannot deliver, and they name the three ways it fails. Then pick the approach with `three-approaches-to-valuation` and `contingent-claim-valuation`, and fix what you are trying to estimate with `value-vs-price-gap`. `narrative-numbers-bridge` states the discipline that everything after it enforces: every number needs a story, every story needs a number.

The spine itself is `story-to-numbers-process`, and its steps expand into their own concepts in order. `landscape-survey` is step 1, and it produces both the business-model map and the cleaned base-year numbers. Step 2 writes the narrative. Step 3 uses two testing concepts. `possible-plausible-probable` grades each claim and routes it to the right device. `narrative-consistency-checks` screens for the impossible, the implausible and the improbable. `life-cycle-uncertainty` sits alongside them. It tells you which uncertainty dominates at this company's stage, and therefore which inputs deserve your effort. Step 4 is `narrative-to-value-drivers`, where each claim becomes one lever. Step 5 runs the model. Step 6 is `narrative-updating-feedback-loop`, which sends you back around.

The two rings are the failure modes and the uncertainty tools. `runaway-stories` and `big-market-delusion` are the narrative pathologies to screen for. The first shows up inside one company. The second is only visible when you aggregate across a sector. `narrative-scenario-grids` and `monte-carlo-valuation-simulation` turn a single value into a range. Use discrete named stories when the doubt is about *which* story. Use distributions when it is about *how much*. Both feed back into the feedback-loop concept, since a narrative shift is what they quantify.

Finally, `uber-narrative-valuation` and `tesla-motley-fool-valuation` are the two end-to-end worked examples. Read Uber for a young private company where market definition dominates, and Tesla for a mature-growth listed company where scaling and margins dominate. Each one exercises every concept on the spine in sequence, so they are the best test of whether the rest has been absorbed.
