## p.9 — Measuring investment returns: ROE, ROC, return spreads, EVA
**Teaches:** Compares actual returns on investment against hurdle rates: ROE vs cost of equity and after-tax ROC vs cost of capital (both on net book values). Three of four firms earn large positive excess returns; Tyson does not (ROC−WACC = −0.77%, EVA −$128m), and the failure to beat its cost of capital is suggested as a driver of its Hillshire acquisition. WACC here varies 6.13% (MCD) to 6.58% (SBUX). Next section previews assessing debt benefits (tax benefit, discipline, bankruptcy cost) and estimating the WACC-minimizing optimal capital structure.
**Formulas:** ROE = Net income / Book equity. After-tax ROC = EBIT*(1 − t) / (Book debt + Book equity − Cash). Equity spread = ROE − Cost of equity. Capital spread = ROC − WACC. EVA = (ROC − WACC) * Capital invested.

## p.13 — Stressed schedule (cont.); debt ratios vs industry and regression; mechanics of moving to the optimal
**Teaches:** Completes the stressed WACC table (70-90% rows). Under stress the impact is most pronounced for Tyson (its optimum falls from 60% to 30%) given its EBIT volatility — supporting a gradual move to the optimum. The team also benchmarks each firm's market debt ratio against its industry average and against an expected debt ratio predicted from a cross-sectional regression on worldwide company data. Starbucks and Chipotle sit far below both benchmarks; McDonald's and Tyson sit above the regression prediction. With stable earnings, low insider ownership, and low intangibles, all four could comfortably run above industry-average leverage. But Tyson's management may rightly fear adverse rating actions from deviating further above the expected ratio. McDonald's stable earnings and size give it more room to borrow. Starbucks and Chipotle face little rating risk. ## p.18 — FCFF valuation results: value per share vs market price
**Teaches:** Executes the DCF. Current FCFF = EBIT*(1−t) − Reinvestment. The PVs of growth-phase and stable-phase cash flows sum to operational assets. Add cash and non-operating investments; subtract debt, minority interest, and the value of equity options to get equity value. Divide by shares outstanding for value per share. Results vs market: McDonald's DCF value ($97.96) is close to its price ($98.23) — fairly valued. Starbucks ($42.57 vs $49.78) and especially Chipotle ($408.50 vs $633.82) trade well above the estimates. The team attributes this to the market expecting higher growth than assumed. Tyson ($53.43 vs $49.78) trades slightly below the estimate, attributed to uncertainty over how the Hillshire acquisition plays out in earnings.
