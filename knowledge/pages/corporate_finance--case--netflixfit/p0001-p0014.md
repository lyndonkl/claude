## p.2 — Case setting and proposal: Netflix Fit (facts 1-3)

**Teaches:** Netflix's US subscriber growth is plateauing, so CEO Reed Hastings is considering "Netflix Fit," a Peloton-modeled equipment-plus-subscription fitness service offering equipment and fitness videos. Key facts begin here. (1) R&D Expenses: Netflix has ALREADY spent and expensed $250 million on fitness equipment and video R&D; none is recoupable if the project is rejected — this is a sunk cost. (2) Introductory Costs: going ahead requires $2.4 billion up front (at time 0) for manufacturing facilities, depreciable straight-line over 10 years down to a salvage value of $400 million. (3) Market Potential and Share: 20 million potential global subscribers, growing 2% a year in perpetuity. Netflix expects 5% market share next year. Share rises 5 percentage points per year — 10% in year 2, 15% in year 3, 20% in year 4 — to a target 25% by year 5, maintained thereafter (continues on p.3).
**Rules:** The $250M R&D is sunk (already expensed, unrecoverable) — should not enter incremental analysis. Straight-line depreciation = (cost − salvage)/life = (2,400 − 400)/10 = $200M per year (implied).
**Compute:** MIXED — subscriber counts and depreciation are deterministic from the stated inputs; treatment of sunk costs is a judgment/principle.
**Tags:** sunk-cost, capital-expenditure, straight-line-depreciation, market-share, netflix-fit

## p.4 — Facts 8-11: studio capacity, G&A allocation, advertising, working capital

**Teaches:** (8) Studio Costs: Netflix's Mumbai studio will be only 40% used next year by Asian entertainment content, which grows 20% a year (studio need growing proportionately). Netflix Fit would use 30% of studio capacity next year (excess capacity — an opportunity-cost issue); when the capacity limit is reached Netflix must invest substantially in a new studio of equivalent capacity, currently estimated at $500 million, growing at inflation. (9) G&A: Netflix will ALLOCATE 4% of existing G&A to the new division. Existing G&A is $1.5 billion firm-wide in the most recent year and grows 5%/year for 10 years with or without the project — hence the allocation is not incremental. IN ADDITION there is a truly incremental $50 million G&A increase next year, growing with the division's dollar revenues thereafter. (10) Advertising: Netflix spent $2 billion on selling/advertising in the most recent year. Without Netflix Fit it grows 5%/year for 10 years. With Netflix Fit, total advertising in years 1-10 is 6% higher than it would otherwise be. The incremental cost is that 6% difference. (11) Working Capital: accounts receivable = 5% of equipment revenues, inventory = 10% of equipment revenues, accounts payable = 5% of equipment revenues, each year.
**Rules:** Allocated 4% of existing G&A is NOT incremental (exists with or without the project); the $50M+ growth piece IS incremental. Incremental advertising_t = 6% x (2,000 x 1.05^t). Net working capital = (5% + 10% − 5%) x equipment revenues = 10% of equipment revenues. Excess studio capacity is free only until combined entertainment (40% growing 20%/yr) + Fit (30%) usage hits 100%; then a $500M-grown-at-inflation studio outlay is triggered.
**Formulas:** NWC_t = 0.05 x EquipRev_t + 0.10 x EquipRev_t − 0.05 x EquipRev_t = 0.10 x EquipRev_t. Incremental advertising_t = 0.06 x 2,000 x (1.05)^t (years 1-10, $ millions).
**Compute:** MIXED — advertising increments, NWC, and studio-capacity trigger year are deterministic given the assumptions; deciding what is incremental (allocated G&A, opportunity cost of studio capacity) is judgment/principle.
**Tags:** incremental-cash-flows, opportunity-cost, allocated-costs, working-capital, advertising

## p.7 — Questions on the project

**Teaches:** The three analytical questions. (1) Accounting Return Analysis: estimate operating income from Netflix Fit over 10 years; estimate the after-tax return on capital over the 10-year period; accept/reject based on after-tax ROC vs. cost of capital — requires consistent allocation/expensing assumptions. (2) Cash Flow Analysis: estimate after-tax incremental cash flows over 10 years. If the project terminates at end of year 10, with working capital and other assets sold at book value, estimate NPV, develop an NPV profile, and estimate the IRR. Then redo NPV, the NPV profile, and IRR assuming a life much longer than 10 years, with reasonable assumptions about reinvestment and cash flows. (3) Business Model: subscription renewal rates are a key driver — state the base-case renewal assumption and test sensitivity of the conclusion to it. Final deliverable: an explicit accept/reject decision with brief explanation.
**Rules:** Accept if after-tax return on capital > cost of capital; accept if NPV > 0 / IRR > cost of capital. Terminal assumption for 10-year case: WC and other assets recovered at book value at end of year 10.
**Compute:** MIXED — ROC, NPV, IRR are deterministic once assumptions are fixed; the allocation, life, and renewal-rate assumptions are judgment.
**Tags:** npv, irr, return-on-capital, sensitivity-analysis, project-questions
