## p.2 — Discrete and Continuous Time

**Teaches:** Cash-flow timing conventions differ across three "worlds." In the real world, most cash flows (revenues, operating expenses) occur continuously over the entire year, possibly with seasonal factors, while some cash flows (tax payments, debt payments) occur at discrete intervals (every quarter or month). In the present-value (PV) world, we use a discrete-time convention: cash flows are assumed to occur at a point in time (e.g., start of the year = Time 0, end of the year = Time 1). In the accounting world, revenues, earnings and expenses are recorded over fiscal years. The diagram shows a one-year timeline from "start of the year (Today)" at Time = 0 to "end of the year" at Time = 1. This frames why annual cash flows in a capital-budgeting model are placed at year-end points even though they actually accrue over the year.

## p.3 — Summary of Conclusions

**Teaches:** The Netflix Fit project's bottom line. On a stand-alone basis the project adds value in the finite-life case, but only marginally; counting synergy benefits to Netflix Entertainment makes it clearly better. Average return on capital in the finite-life case is 10.76% without synergy and about 14.08% with synergy — the latter exceeds Netflix Fit's cost of capital of 8.01%. ## p.4 — Cost of Capital Derivation (Bottom-Up Beta Flowchart)

**Teaches:** A full bottom-up cost-of-capital derivation for Netflix Fit and Netflix Entertainment. Start with other firms in the fitness business (median regression beta 1.25, median D/E 34.92%, median cash/value 5.26%). Unlever the beta and correct for cash. Then relever at Netflix's own market debt-to-equity ratio to get the project beta. Netflix's market equity = $368.77 x 438.81 shares = $161,820 m; market debt = PV of leases $15,161 m + $1,727 m = $16,888 m, giving D/E = 16,888/161,820 = 10.44% and D/C = 9.45%. Pre-tax cost of debt = riskfree 1.5% + spread 2% = 3.5%; marginal tax rate = 25%; equity risk premium = 6.27%.

**Formulas:**
- Unlevered beta = Regression beta / (1 + (1 - t) x (D/E)) = 1.25/(1 + (1 - .25)(.3492)) = 0.99, where t = marginal tax rate, D/E = median debt-to-equity of comparable fitness firms.
- Unlevered beta corrected for cash = Unlevered beta / (1 - cash/value) = 0.99/(1 - .0526) = 1.0495.
- Levered beta for Netflix Fit = Cash-corrected unlevered beta x (1 + (1 - t)(Netflix D/E)) = 1.0495 x (1 + (1 - .25)(.1044)) = 1.1274.
- Cost of equity = Riskfree rate + Levered beta x Equity risk premium: Netflix Fit = 1.50% + 1.1274(6.27%) = 8.57%; Netflix Entertainment (levered beta 1.29) = 1.50% + 1.29(6.27%) = 9.59%.
- Cost of capital = Cost of equity x (E/(D+E)) + Pre-tax cost of debt x (1 - t) x (D/(D+E)). Netflix Fit: 8.57%(0.9055) + 3.50%(1 - .25)(.0945) = 8.01%. Netflix Entertainment: 9.59%(.9039) + 3.5%(1 - .25)(.0961) = 8.93%.

## p.8 — Some Thoughts on Operating Income

**Teaches:** Three cautions about accounting operating income in project analysis. (1) Multiple cost-allocation mechanisms can be used to compute operating income, and return on capital is affected by allocation choices. (2) Depreciation choices have profound effects on return on capital — a more accelerated depreciation method would raise ROC substantially (lower book capital and shifted income). (3) Operating income is computed after marginal taxes, because the project's income is incremental and taxed at the marginal rate. It also excludes the tax savings from interest expense. Financing effects belong in the cost of capital (the discount rate), not the cash flows. Including them in both would double count.

**Rules:** Tax project operating income at the marginal tax rate; never include interest tax savings in operating cash flows (they are captured in the after-tax cost of debt within the cost of capital).
**Compute:** JUDGMENT (methodological principles about allocation, depreciation, and tax treatment).

## p.15 — Explanations for Infinite Life Case

**Teaches:** How assumptions change when the project life is extended to infinity. Capital maintenance can no longer be zero: capital expenditures are set to exceed depreciation by 1% (the inflation rate) throughout the 10 years — whatever book-value depletion depreciation causes is made up by new maintenance capex with an inflation adjustment. In year 11, capital expenditures are set above depreciation using the 10-year compounded inflation adjustment (= 200 x 1.01^10), because in perpetuity the assets must be kept pristine to support 1%-a-year growth forever. The synergy benefits are also assumed to continue in perpetuity.

**Formulas:** Maintenance capex_t = Depreciation_t x (1 + inflation) with inflation = 1%; Year-11 capex benchmark = 200 x 1.01^10.
**Rules:** If a project is assumed to last forever with growth g = inflation, capital maintenance must exceed depreciation by the inflation rate; growth without reinvestment is inconsistent.
**Compute:** MIXED — the capex adjustments are deterministic given the 1% inflation rate; deciding that perpetual life requires them is judgment.
**Tags:** infinite-life, capital-maintenance, inflation, perpetuity, reinvestment-consistency

## p.17 — The Terminal Value: Assumptions and Calculation

**Teaches:** How the infinite-life terminal value is set. After year 10, the number of subscribers is assumed level, but subscription prices grow at the 1% inflation rate, so cash flows grow at g = 1% forever. Year-11 cash flow ($244.33 m) is much lower than year-10 cash flow because there are no more add-on subscribers (no growth capex offsets, steady state). Terminal value is a growing perpetuity of the year-11 cash flow at Netflix Fit's cost of capital.

**Formulas:** Terminal Value = CF in year 11 / (Cost of capital - g) = 244.33 / (0.0801 - 0.01) = $3,486 million, where CF year 11 = steady-state FCFF, cost of capital = 8.01%, g = 1% (inflation).
**Rules:** The terminal-value cash flow must be a steady-state number (no add-on subscriber growth); perpetuity growth is capped at the inflation rate here.
**Example:** TV = 244.33/(.0801 - .01) = $3,486 m, placed at end of year 10 in the infinite-life FCFF table.

## p.20 — Consistency in Growth and Investment Assumptions

**Teaches:** A consistency framework linking the assumed life/growth of a project after the explicit forecast period ("after year 15" in the slide's generic framing) to the required capital-expenditure assumption. Growth assumptions and reinvestment assumptions must match: you cannot assume perpetual growth while spending nothing on the asset base.

**Rules:**
| Life/growth assumption | Required capital expenditure assumption |
|---|---|
| Project ends | No (or very low) capital maintenance; let assets run down toward end of life |
| Infinite life; g = 0% | Capital maintenance = Depreciation; maintain invested capital at base level |
| Infinite life; g = inflation | Capital maintenance > Depreciation; invested capital has to grow at the inflation rate |
| Infinite life; g > inflation | Capital investment to increase capacity; capital maintenance > depreciation; invested capital has to grow to reflect real growth |
**Compute:** JUDGMENT — a classification rule set for choosing internally consistent assumptions.
**Tags:** growth-consistency, capital-maintenance, reinvestment, terminal-value-assumptions, heuristics
